An engine oil cooler takes heat out of the oil before it goes back through the engine. The air-to-oil kind works like a little radiator. Hot oil runs through it and air carries the heat away. If you're doing lap after lap, that helps keep the oil from getting too hot and thinning out. In the video I show the parts that make that loop work.

## Where the oil goes

On a setup like this, the sandwich plate goes between the block and the oil filter. The filter screws onto the plate, and the plate gives you two ports for the oil lines. The engine's oil pump sends oil out through one line and into the cooler. Inside, the oil passes through channels that transfer heat into the metal fins. Air moving across the fins carries that heat away, and the other line brings the oil back to the plate.

## What happens when the oil is cold

You want the oil to warm up when you first start the car. In a thermostatic setup, cold oil has a shorter path that bypasses the cooler. As the oil warms up, the thermostat starts closing that shortcut and sends more oil through the cooler. The temperature where that happens depends on the thermostat.

If you're figuring out whether you need a cooler, start by looking at oil temperature during the kind of driving you actually do. If it keeps climbing over a track session, a cooler gives the oil another way to shed heat. Give the fins a path for air and run both oil lines clear of the exhaust and sharp edges.
