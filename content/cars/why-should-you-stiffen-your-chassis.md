Stiffening the chassis on an older Honda can make the steering feel more direct by keeping the suspension mounts from moving around as much in a corner. The first brace I'd put on an EF Honda is a front strut tower bar. On my CRX I could feel the difference as soon as I turned into a corner. In the video I install and compare a few braces so you can see what bolts up under the hood and what goes underneath the car.

## What the front bar does

When you turn into a corner, the outside suspension takes more load. Some of that load goes into the sheet metal around the towers. If the towers move relative to each other, the suspension is working from mounting points that are moving too. A tower bar gives that load a path from one tower to the other and helps hold the tops of the towers in place.

Think about a quick left-right turn. The load shifts from one side to the other, which is where a little extra movement in the body can make the car feel less direct. A brace changes how much the body moves. Your springs and shocks still control how the wheels move up and down.

Pop the hood and you can see both ends of a front tower bar. It spans the engine bay and bolts up at the top of each tower. You can do the job from above with basic hand tools, which is part of why I'd start there. A rear tower bar does the same job at the back of the car.

## Braces underneath the car

A lower tie bar joins points underneath the car. The Ultra Racing bar below goes at the rear, near the lower control arms. A front lower bar can tie together the lower mounting area up front. When you're comparing parts, look at where each end bolts up. A rear tower bar braces the top of the body, while a rear lower bar braces the area where the control arms attach.

![Ultra Racing rear lower strut bar](/img/why-should-you-stiffen-your-chassis/2.jpg)
*Ultra Racing rear lower bar*

The bars underneath are more work mostly because you have to get to the bolts from below. Look at the mounting points and where the bar will sit before you order one, especially if the car is lowered and you already have to think about speed bumps.

For a street car I'd stop at a front tower bar and one or two braces underneath. Drive the car between installs. If you bolt everything on at once, it's hard to tell which part made a difference.
