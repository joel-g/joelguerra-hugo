The Big 3 are the three main wiring connections in a car's charging system. They run from the alternator to battery positive, from battery negative to the body, and from the engine to the body.

1. **Alternator to battery positive.** This is the thick wire on the back of the alternator. It carries current to the battery and the rest of the car, usually through a main fuse or fusible link.
2. **Battery negative to the body.** This cable bolts to the sheet metal and connects it to the battery's negative post. Things like the headlights can ground to the body instead of running a separate wire all the way back to the battery.
3. **Engine to the body.** The alternator usually grounds through its case to the engine. The engine sits on mounts that usually have rubber in them, so there's a cable or braided strap to connect the engine to the body. Some cars run a ground from the engine straight to battery negative.

The "big" part is how much of the car depends on those connections. The charge wire carries the alternator's output, and the grounds give current a way back. On a car wired this way, the body and engine carry current between the ground cables. The headlights, radiator fan, and other electrical loads share parts of that path.

A little resistance at a loose or crusty connection can eat up some of the voltage and affect every electrical component in the car. The more current you pull through it, the bigger the drop. That's how you can have an alternator that works fine and a battery that still charges poorly. A bad ground can cause the same problem as a bad connection on the charge wire.
