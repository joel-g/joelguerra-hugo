The coolant overflow reservoir holds the extra coolant the radiator pushes out as the engine warms up, then supplies it back as the engine cools. Coolant travels both ways through the little hose between the radiator and the bottle. In the video I swap the plastic bottle for a metal one from Evil Energy.

## What the radiator cap does

Coolant takes up more room as it heats up, so the pressure in the radiator rises. Once it reaches the cap's rating, a spring-loaded valve opens. Some coolant goes out through the hose into the bottle, and the level in the bottle rises.

After you shut the car off, the coolant contracts as it cools. That drops the pressure in the radiator and opens a second valve in the cap. The radiator draws coolant back through the same hose, so the bottle level drops again.

## What to look for at the bottle

Follow the small hose from the radiator neck to the bottle. Inside the bottle, the pickup for that hose needs to reach below the coolant level. That's usually a tube that reaches down from the top or a fitting near the bottom. When you swap the bottle, that connection matters. If the radiator draws air through a cracked hose or a pickup sitting above the coolant, the bottle can still have coolant in it while the radiator is low.

Check the bottle against its level marks when the engine is cold. If the bottle is empty, check the radiator level too once the engine and radiator have cooled. Leave the radiator cap on while it's hot.
